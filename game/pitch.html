One model that is the game.

You press the buttons. The model plays everything else: the house, every rule, and the monster.

How it plays

A loop, every 50 ms

Your controllerstick · creep · blow · use · throw

Screen + soundtoday's renderer, unchanged
  • It runs 20 times a second, and the screen smooths it to 60 fps.
  • It predicts the game state, not pixels. That's cheaper, needs less data, and lets us test each rule.
  • It runs in the browser, on the laptop.
What the model sees

The game, as about 115 small pieces

map ×60 patches kid monster body monster mind mood · plan · where it thinks you are ×4 candles ×6 doors ×11 wardrobes ×3 toys ×7 squeaky toys ×5 gaps ×10 searchlight hand clock monster type your input
  • Each piece is a short list of numbers: where it is, how it's moving, whether it's on or off.
  • The monster's mind is part of the state, so the model needs no hidden memory.
Inside the model

Two steps each tick

1

The monster decides

The monster head reads the state and presses the giant's buttons: move, run, lunge, open, smash, yell, searchlight, hand.

2

The world moves

The core takes both controllers and plays the tick. Out comes the next state, plus what happened ("candle out", "door smashed").

  • A transformer: every piece looks at every other piece.
  • That is how it learns "kid next to a lit candle + blowing → candle goes out".
  • Small: about 6 million numbers (8 layers, width 256).
Randomness

Reasonable, never wild

  • The model gives odds, not one answer, and we draw from them.
  • A move is one of 64 bins, so "it might go left or right" can both be true.
  • A temperature sets how adventurous the draw is.
  • The monster's mood raises the temperature: a frustrated monster tries new things, a confident one stays steady.
The monster

Five types, one goal: catch the kid

Each game jitters its type's settings, so no two games play the same. Each type is tuned so a decent kid wins 5–25% of games.

Its feelings have reasons

A "how am I doing" score drives its mood

anger · runs, smashes, yells
frustration · tries another way
confidence · steady, predictable
FURY: 4 s rampage, then exhausted

Every yell is logged with its reason. The same score can later be the reward for RL.

Every case shows up

Data that covers every rule

  • Kid bots in 5 styles: Sneaker, Sprinter, Hider, Trickster, Greedy. Each has a skill level.
  • 5% chaos kids press random buttons, so the model learns what does not happen.
  • 30% of games start mid-game, so late moments (the hand, the last candle) are common.
  • A coverage check counts every rule in docs/mechanics.md: at least 1,000 each.
  • Any game replays exactly and can be watched in the browser.
Training (next step, its own plan)

Teach it, then test it like an exam

  • Step 1: predict one tick ahead.
  • Step 2: play 32 ticks on its own predictions, so small errors don't snowball.
  • Exam: every rule as a mini test, model vs the real rules.
  • Sanity: 5-minute games where nobody walks through walls and candles never relight.
  • Final test: you play it.
