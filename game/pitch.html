GameNet: the plan

One model is the whole game. You press the buttons; it plays everything else.

1. The model: what goes in, what comes out

One model. Each tick it reads the game as 115 pieces: the game cut into small parts, each a short list of numbers. It writes out the next moment.

Each tick, the same model runs twice:

  1. Pass 1: the giant's buttons are left empty. The giant-buttons head decides what the monster presses: move, run, lunge, open, smash, yell, light, hand.
  2. Pass 2: your input and those buttons go in. Every other head writes the next moment and the events.
  3. The screen draws it, and the next moment becomes the input. Twenty times a second.

One piece up close: the kid.

in (13 numbers)out (next moment)
position x, z · velocity · facing · crawling · running · creeping · hidden in · holding · blowing · noisehow far it moved (odds over 64 bins per axis) · each yes/no as a chance · the rest as numbers

Training (later, its own plan):

  • Pass 2 gets the bot's real giant buttons, and its outputs are compared with what the real rules did.
  • The giant-buttons head learns to press what the bots pressed.
  • Then it plays 32 ticks on its own predictions, so small errors don't snowball.

No hidden memory. The monster's mind (mood, plan, guesses where you are) is part of the pieces, so one moment in is enough.

2. The monster

  • Five types, one goal: catch the kid.
    • Pathfinder (dumb baseline)
    • Brute (charges and smashes)
    • Stalker (predicts you and cuts you off)
    • Hound (hunts by ear, easy to lure)
    • Warden (guards the last candles)
  • Each game jitters its type's settings, so no two games play the same.
  • A "how am I doing" score drives its mood:
    • a vent escape, a door in its face, a candle out → anger and frustration;
    • spotting you → confidence.
  • What mood does:
    • anger makes it run, smash and yell;
    • frustration makes it try another way round;
    • both high → a 4 s fury, then exhausted (your chance).
  • Randomness is reasonable: it always picks among sensible moves, more boldly when frustrated. Every yell is logged with its reason.

3. The data

  • Kid bots vs monster bots play the real rules (no graphics). We record every piece, 20 times a second.
  • Kid bots in 5 styles (Sneaker, Sprinter, Hider, Trickster, Greedy) at different skill levels.
  • 5% chaos kids press random buttons, so the model learns what does not happen.
  • 30% of games start mid-game, so late moments (the hand, the last candle) are common.
  • A coverage check counts every rule in docs/mechanics.md: at least 1,000 each.
  • Any game replays exactly and can be watched in the browser.
  • First batch: 2,000 games ≈ 80 h of play ≈ 5.8 M moments, made in under 30 min on the laptop.
